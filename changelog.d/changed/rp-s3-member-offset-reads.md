- **Offset, property and class-constant reads are judged wherever they are written, not only as the whole right-hand side of `=` or a `return` operand, and each finding now points at the read itself instead of the start of the statement.** `offset.missing`, `offset.on-unsupported`, `offset.undeclared`, `offset.maybe-missing`, `property.undefined`, `property.maybe-undefined`, `property.on-non-object`, `property.inaccessible`, `class-const.undefined` and `class-const.inaccessible` now judge a read in an `echo`, an operator, a cast, an interpolation, an array element, a comparison or the read half of `++` or `.=`, every level of a chain such as `$a['n']['k']`, and an argument of a function whose parameter is provably by value, so findings can appear where they were silent. A read PHP does not warn about stays silent: an argument that may be by reference (which creates the key or the property), an argument of a method or constructor, an arm of a ternary or the right of `&&`, `||` and `??` (for the value-dependent ids), an `@` operand (for the warnings), a read whose variable the same statement may rebind first, and an extent a decided `defined()` guard proves dead; a class constant still reports under `??` and `@`, where PHP throws. A finding that already existed keeps its id and message but moves to the read's column (`$x = $a['k'];` reports at `$a['k']`), so a baseline keyed by column needs one reseed. Separately, a guard such as `isset($d['url'])` on a key a declared sealed shape cannot carry no longer leaves the guarded read reported as outside the declaration. On the ten public packages one strict finding moves column and nothing else changes (#936, #949, #1050).
