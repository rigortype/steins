- **A `php` wrapper script that does not `exec` the interpreter no longer hangs `steins check`.** When the `php` on `PATH` was a shell script running the real interpreter as its own child (`/path/to/php "$@"`, a common shape for version managers and container shims), every close of a sidecar child (the end of a run, a timed-out request, a replaced child, a failed boot handshake) killed only the wrapper and then waited forever on the interpreter, which still held the child's output open. A close now drops the child's stdin, kills the child's whole process group on Unix (the child leads a group of its own), and stops waiting for the child's output after half a second, so no close can block a run. On 0.1.8 and earlier, make the wrapper `exec` the interpreter. Findings do not change.
